Metal-Acid Reaction Dynamics

Observe the microscopic differences when a metal reacts with an acid to form an insoluble salt (which acts as a physical barrier) versus a soluble salt (which dissolves, allowing continuous reaction).

Reaction A Insoluble Salt

Passivation layer forms and blocks reaction

Ca(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CaSO₄(s)↓ + H₂(g)↑
Waiting to drop metal...
Observation: As the reaction proceeds, an insoluble salt layer (e.g., CaSO₄) quickly coats the surface of the metal. This physical barrier prevents the acid molecules from reaching the unreacted metal beneath, causing the bubbling to stop.

Reaction B Soluble Salt

Ions dissolve, continuous reaction

Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) + H₂(g)↑
Waiting to drop metal...
Observation: The salt formed (e.g., ZnSO₄) is highly soluble in water. It immediately dissociates into mobile ions that drift away into the bulk solution. The metal surface remains continuously exposed to the acid until it is fully consumed.
