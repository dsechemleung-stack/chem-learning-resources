Insoluble Layer that Stops Further Reactions

Observe the microscopic differences when a metal/base reacts with acid/CO₂ to form an insoluble product (which acts as a physical barrier) versus a soluble product (which dissolves, allowing continuous reaction).

Reaction A Insoluble Layer

Insoluble layer forms and blocks reaction

Ca(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CaSO₄(s)↓ + H₂(g)↑
Waiting to start reaction...
Observation: As the reaction proceeds, an insoluble product (e.g., CaSO₄) quickly coats the surface of the metal. This insoluble layer prevents the acid molecules from reaching the unreacted metal beneath, causing the bubbling to stop.

Reaction B Soluble Mobile Ions

Ions dissolve, continuous reaction

Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑
Waiting to start reaction...
Observation: The product formed (e.g., ZnSO₄) is highly soluble in water. It immediately dissociates into mobile ions that drift away into the bulk solution. The metal surface remains continuously exposed to the acid until it is fully consumed.
